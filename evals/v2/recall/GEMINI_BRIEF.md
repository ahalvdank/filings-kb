## PART 1: write 30 questions

You're helping build an evaluation set for a retrieval system over SEC 10-K filings. Your questions will be pooled with 30 written by another model (Claude). The two models then review each other's sets, and the human owner picks the best 30 of the 60. The numbers this produces get published on a public portfolio site, so the questions must be fair, natural and answerable.

### The system

- **Corpus:** a public sample of 61 10-K filings: 12 US companies × 5 fiscal years, plus one 10-K/A. Companies: AMD, AMZN (Amazon), BA (Boeing), CVX (Chevron), GOOGL (Alphabet), INTC (Intel), JPM (JPMorgan Chase), META, MSFT (Microsoft), NVDA (NVIDIA), UNH (UnitedHealth), XOM (Exxon Mobil). Fiscal years are each company's own (NVIDIA FY2026 ended January 2026; Microsoft FY2026 ended June 2026). Most companies cover FY2021–FY2025; NVDA and MSFT cover FY2022–FY2026.
- **Sections:** each filing is split into its 10-K Items (Item 1 Business, Item 1A Risk Factors, Item 3 Legal Proceedings, Item 7 MD&A, Item 8 Financial Statements and Notes, and so on).
- **Passages:** each section is cut into passages of whole sentences, about 2,000 characters on average (roughly 400–500 tokens).
- **Retriever under test:** dense embeddings (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) in a Chroma vector store. A query is always **filtered to one (company, fiscal year, section)**, and the retriever ranks every passage in that section by cosine similarity to the question text. The question goes in as-is: no rewriting, no keyword expansion.
- **What the eval measures:** recall@k. Of the passages in that section that a human judges relevant to the question, what fraction does the retriever rank in its top k (k = 5, 10, 20)? Gold passages come from a pool: the retriever's top 5 plus a keyword (BM25) search's top 5. The human labels each pooled passage relevant or not.

### Rules for each question

1. **One section only.** Name the ticker, fiscal year and section. The answer must be in that section of that filing. No comparisons across years or companies.
2. **Pick from the eligible sections listed below.** They all have at least 20 passages; prefer those with 30 or more, because ranking matters more there. Avoid sections not on the list: some are one-passage stubs where the company incorporated the content by reference (Chevron, JPMorgan and Exxon MD&A and financial statements), and some Intel sections are mislabelled by the section detector.
3. **Ask like an analyst, not like the filing.** Use natural wording. Don't copy distinctive phrases from the topic hints below or from a 10-K. A question that repeats the passage's exact wording tests string matching, not retrieval.
4. **It must be answerable from the text.** Ask about something the section actually discloses. The topic hints after each section come from claims extracted from that section, so they're a reliable guide to what's there. Don't invent events.
5. **Mix two kinds,** about half each:
   - `fact`: one specific disclosure (a date, an amount, a ruling, a named program), likely in 1–2 passages.
   - `thematic`: a topic discussed across several passages (for example, how a company describes its supply-chain risk).
6. **Spread:** cover at least 10 of the 12 companies and at least 4 section types, put no more than 4 questions on one company, and include at least 6 questions from fiscal years before the latest.
7. **One question per line,** without the answer. Don't put more than one question inside a single question.
